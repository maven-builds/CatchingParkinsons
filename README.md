# Catching Parkinson's
# How Can We Quantify Prodromal Dysfunction To Enable Objective Clinical Auditing?

A research project focused on examining speech and motor dysfunction in patients diagnosed with Parkinson's disease, idiopathic RBD (REM Sleep Behaviour Disorder), or a similar motor disorder.

Current Phase: Data Processing

# Brief Overview

Parkinson's disease is a motor disorder of the nervous system, in the family of synucleinopathies. 
Synucleinopathies are a group of motor disorders, such as Dementia with Lewy Bodies and Multiple System Atrophy, that are characterized by the accumulation of the protein alpha-synuclein.
While Parkinson's disease is incurable, and the average age of formal diagnosis is 60+, key indicators often show up several years earlier.

One of the most glaring indicators is idiopathic RBD, a case of RBD not clearly attributed to external factors such as medication. Patients of RBD have an ~80% conversion rate to a synucleinopathy.
Idiopathic RBD usually occurs over 10 to 20 years prior to the formal diagnosis of a motor disorder. Hence, it is one of the largest areas of focus for researchers.

The goal of this project is to analyze dysfunction in the prodromal window of Parkinson's disease to reduce reliance on subjective clinical auditing.
